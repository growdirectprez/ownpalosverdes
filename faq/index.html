Living Here

Living on the Palos Verdes Peninsula

Is Palos Verdes one city or several?

The Palos Verdes Peninsula is four separate cities — Palos Verdes Estates, Rancho Palos Verdes, Rolling Hills Estates, and Rolling Hills — plus distinct neighborhoods within each. Which city you are in affects price, feel, and sometimes schools. See the neighborhood guide →

What is Palos Verdes known for?

Sweeping ocean and city-light views, top-rated public schools, and an unusual amount of open space — over 400 acres of nature reserve and roughly 12 miles of coastline. It is a quiet, low-density peninsula 25–35 minutes from LAX yet a world away from it. More on life here →

How far is Palos Verdes from LA, LAX, and the beach?

LAX is about 25 to 35 minutes by car and Downtown LA roughly 35 to 50 minutes depending on traffic. The beaches are on the Peninsula itself — the coves and tide pools are minutes from most neighborhoods. The area is car-dependent with little public transit.

Is Palos Verdes safe?

The Peninsula has consistently ranked among the lowest-crime areas in Los Angeles County. Rolling Hills is a guard-gated private city, and the other three cities are quiet, residential, and low-traffic. Specifics vary block to block.

What is the weather like on the Palos Verdes Peninsula?

Mild and coastal year-round — cooler and breezier than inland LA and rarely very hot. Expect a May Gray and June Gloom marine layer that burns off by midday in early summer, and clear, temperate afternoons most of the year.

Schools

Palos Verdes Schools (PVPUSD)

How good are Palos Verdes schools?

PVPUSD is one of the reasons many families move to the Peninsula — consistently among the highest-performing public districts in the South Bay. For most buyers with kids, the assigned school is worth checking before you fall for a house. Full school guide →

What schools are in the Palos Verdes school district?

PVPUSD runs boundary-based elementary schools (TK–5), three intermediate campuses for grades 6–8, and two comprehensive high schools — Palos Verdes High and Peninsula High — plus strong private and parochial options. School guide →

Do you have to live in Palos Verdes to attend PVPUSD?

Generally yes — enrollment is residency-based, and your address determines your assigned elementary and intermediate school. The most common out-of-district path is the grandparent permit (TK–12, when the student has a grandparent residing in the district). Verify any address against the district boundary tool first. More →

How does school assignment and choice work in PVPUSD?

Elementary (TK–5) and intermediate (6–8) are boundary-assigned by home address. High school is open choice — any resident student may attend either Palos Verdes High or Peninsula High. Boundaries can shift in strong enrollment years, so always confirm a specific address. School guide →

Which Palos Verdes neighborhoods feed which schools?

Feeder patterns follow the city and neighborhood, and elementary boundaries are drawn street by street. Rather than generalize, I will pull the exact assigned schools for any specific address you are considering. School guide →

Neighborhoods

Palos Verdes Neighborhoods

What is the difference between PVE, RPV, RHE, and Rolling Hills?

Palos Verdes Estates is the oldest and most architecturally controlled. Rancho Palos Verdes is the largest and most varied, with many big ocean-view areas. Rolling Hills Estates is horse-friendly and central. Rolling Hills is a guard-gated, ranch-style private city. Neighborhood guide →

Which Palos Verdes neighborhoods have ocean views?

View properties cluster along the south and west faces of the Hill — Lunada Bay and Malaga Cove in PVE, and Mira Catalina, La Cresta, Los Verdes, and South Shores in RPV, among others. Ocean view ranges from wide panoramas to peeks. Explore neighborhoods →

What are the more affordable areas of Palos Verdes?

Relative to the estate neighborhoods, condos and townhomes (parts of RPV and Rolling Hills Estates) and homes toward the Peninsula edges tend to be the entry points. Affordable is relative here, but there is a real range. See prices by city →

Buying & the Market

Buying a Home in Palos Verdes

How much does a house in Palos Verdes cost?

As of the latest month, the median closed single-family price on the Peninsula was about $1.86M, and over the past year the monthly median has run roughly $1.86M–$2.34M. Prices vary widely by city and view — from condos below the median to oceanfront estates well above it. See the monthly market reports for the current figure.

Is the Palos Verdes market competitive right now?

Moderately. In the latest month, homes went under contract in a median of about 21 days across 67 closed sales. Well-priced, view, and turnkey homes move fastest; dated or over-priced listings sit. The monthly reports track this in real numbers.

What are property taxes like in Palos Verdes?

Under California Proposition 13 the base property tax is roughly 1 percent of a home assessed value, plus local voter-approved assessments and any Mello-Roos, so effective rates typically land a bit above 1 percent. A home is reassessed to its purchase price when you buy. This is general information, not tax advice.

Are there HOAs in Palos Verdes?

Most single-family homes on the Peninsula are not in an HOA. The big exception is Rolling Hills, a guard-gated private city with its own association; some condo and townhome communities carry HOA dues. I always flag any HOA, dues, and rules on a specific property. Neighborhood guide →

Lifestyle & Practical

Daily Life on the Hill

What is there to do in Palos Verdes?

A lot of it is outdoors: five golf courses, 400+ acres of reserve with hiking and horse trails, rocky coves and tide pools, and miles of coastline. Add equestrian facilities, art and garden centers, local theater, junior guards, surf camp, and youth sports. Life on the Hill →

Do you need a car in Palos Verdes? Is it walkable?

You will want a car. The Peninsula is residential and low-density by design — retail sits in small commercial pockets rather than long strips, and homes are spread across winding hillside roads. A few neighborhoods have walkable cores, but daily life is car-based. More →

What should I know before moving to Palos Verdes?

A few honest things: it is car-dependent with winding roads, cell coverage can be spotty in the canyons and along the reserve, and the coastal marine layer means gray mornings in early summer. In exchange: space, quiet, views, top schools, and open land. Life on the Hill →
